===============================================================================
1. WHAT VERIPIC IS
===============================================================================

VeriPic is an Android application, written in Flutter/Dart, that captures
photographs in a way that makes later tampering detectable.

When you take a photo with VeriPic, three things happen that an ordinary camera
does not do:

  1. The GPS coordinates, altitude, a reverse-geocoded place name, and a UTC
     timestamp are permanently burned into the image pixels as a visible banner.

  2. A cryptographic signature is computed over that data plus a perceptual
     fingerprint of the banner, and embedded into the image file in three
     separate places.

  3. The signing key is derived from the physical device's hardware identifier,
     so a signature can only be produced by that one device.

A second mode of the app takes any image and checks it: it recovers the embedded
payload, validates the signature, and re-computes the perceptual fingerprints of
both the stamp and the photographic scene, comparing them to the recorded ones.
The result is a plain verdict - authentic, edited, mismatched, or unsigned.
There is no probability attached to it: the record either verifies or it does
not.

In one sentence: VeriPic is a camera that produces photographs which can prove
where and when they were taken, and which reveal it if anyone edits them
afterwards.


===============================================================================
2. THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES
===============================================================================

A photograph on its own is no longer evidence of anything. Three separate forces
have eroded its trustworthiness:

  * EXIF metadata is trivially editable. Any free tool can rewrite the GPS
    coordinates and timestamp of a JPEG in seconds, and nothing in the file
    records that it happened.

  * Screenshot-and-crop, clone-stamping, and text editing can change what a
    photo appears to show. A photo of a meter reading, a delivery, or a site
    condition can have its numbers or its stamp altered.

  * Generative AI can now produce photorealistic images of events that never
    occurred, at zero cost.

The consequence is that "send a photo as proof" no longer works for insurance
claims, field inspections, compliance records, or delivery confirmation.

VeriPic addresses this by binding the claim (where, when, which device) to the
pixels (a perceptual fingerprint of the visible stamp) using a key that never
leaves the device that took the photo. Editing either the claim or the stamp
breaks the binding, and the verifier can tell which one broke.


===============================================================================
3. WHO IT IS FOR
===============================================================================

The design direction in CLAUDE.md describes the app as "an instrument, not a
social camera" and specifies that it must be legible in sunlight and usable with
gloves. That points squarely at fieldwork. The intended users are:

  * Field inspectors and surveyors - documenting site conditions where the
    location and time of a photo is the whole point of taking it.

  * Insurance loss adjusters - recording damage in a way that resists later
    dispute about when or where the photo was taken.

  * Compliance and audit staff - producing records that must survive scrutiny.

  * Logistics and delivery - proof-of-delivery photographs tied to a place and
    a moment.

  * Journalists and researchers - establishing provenance of source imagery.

Secondary audience: anyone verifying a photo they received. The verification
flow does not require you to be the person who captured it - though there is an
important limitation on cross-device verification, covered in section 21.

4. WHAT IT DOES - FEATURE BY FEATURE
===============================================================================

CAPTURE
  - Live camera preview with tap-to-focus and exposure point.
  - Torch toggle.
  - Front/rear camera switching.
  - Continuous GPS position stream with accuracy reporting.
  - Live status readout showing fix quality, e.g. "FIX +/-4M". Turns alert
    coloured and reads "WEAK" past 15 m accuracy, or "STALE" if the fix is more
    than 30 seconds old.
  - Live stamp preview card showing exactly what will be burned into the next
    frame: site name, coordinates, altitude, and time.
  - Reverse geocoding of coordinates into a human place name, re-resolved only
    after the operator moves more than 50 m.
  - The shutter refuses to fire without a usable GPS fix, and states the reason
    inline rather than failing silently or throwing.
  - MOCK LOCATION BLOCKING. If the operating system reports the fix came from a
    mock provider (a fake-GPS app), the status readout reads "FIX FAKED -
    BLOCKED" and capture is refused. Signing a spoofed coordinate would make
    VeriPic vouch for a lie, so the frame is never produced.
  - Captured frames are written to disk and exported to a "VeriPic" album in the
    device gallery.

FRAMES GALLERY
  - Scrollable, pull-to-refresh grid of frames captured by this app, newest
    first.
  - Shows only VeriPic's own output - never the device camera roll.
  - Tapping a frame opens it full size with pinch-zoom, alongside the signed
    payload that was sealed into it.

LOCATIONS
  - A real, interactive slippy map: pan, pinch-zoom, double-tap zoom, fling.
  - Every capture is a square outlined pin drawn by the app, so markers follow
    the design system rather than the tile provider's styling. The selected pin
    grows and turns accent-coloured.
  - Tapping a pin, or a row in the list below, GLIDES the map to that capture
    over 180 ms rather than teleporting, so you keep your bearings.
  - Coordinates are read from the signed envelope inside each frame, so a pin
    cannot exist without a valid signed capture behind it.
  - Basemap tiles come from OpenStreetMap: no API key, no billing account, no
    Google Cloud project. Tiles are colour-filtered - desaturated in light mode,
    inverted and warmed in dark mode - so the map sits under the palette instead
    of fighting it.
  - Attribution is displayed, as OSM's licence requires.

VERIFICATION
  - Pick any image from the gallery, or take one with the system camera.
  - Four-stage animated diagnostic run (see section 6).
  - Verdict card stating authentic or not authentic, with no probability
    attached.
  - Stamp drift gauge showing the Hamming distance against the tolerance.
  - Scene integrity grid showing every tile of the photographic content, green
    where it matches the signed original and alert-coloured where it does not,
    so an edit is located rather than merely reported.
  - Expandable drawer of all embedded metadata with tap-to-copy on every field.
  - One-tap export of a draft evidence certificate as a PDF (see below).

EVIDENCE CERTIFICATE EXPORT
  - Generates a PDF laid out to follow the certificate structure required by
    section 63(4) of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 for electronic
    records.
  - Pre-fills the technical particulars the app can attest to: the record, the
    device, the manner of production, and the verification result, plus the
    frame itself.
  - Prompts for the declarant's name, designation, address, and a case
    reference; any left blank prints a ruled line to complete by hand.
  - Leaves both signature blocks (Part A, person in control of the device; Part
    B, person of expert capacity) deliberately blank.
  - Includes an explicit "limits of this certificate" paragraph.
  - IMPORTANT: unsigned, the document has no legal effect, and neither the app
    nor its author provides legal advice or warrants admissibility. See
    section 21.

DEVICE AND KEY DIAGNOSTICS
  - Hardware fingerprint and active signing key id shown on the home screen.
  - Full attribute set (manufacturer, model, board, hardware, build
    fingerprint, OS version) behind a disclosure.
  - Warns explicitly when the key is bound to a stored fallback rather than a
    real hardware identifier.

APPEARANCE
  - Light and dark themes, plus a "follow the device" mode.
  - Choice persists across launches.

5. HOW IT WORKS - THE CAPTURE PIPELINE
===============================================================================

Implemented in lib/services/camera_service.dart, method capture().

  STEP 1 - PARALLEL ACQUISITION
    Three operations start concurrently to keep shutter lag down:
      - c.takePicture()          - the raw frame from the sensor
      - _readPosition()          - a best-accuracy GPS fix
      - _deviceService.getDeviceId() - the public device hash

    _readPosition() first checks Geolocator.isLocationServiceEnabled() and
    throws if the radio is off, so a capture can never silently produce a frame
    with no location.

  STEP 2 - BURN THE STAMP  (lib/services/overlay_service.dart)
    applyGpsStamp() decodes the raw JPEG and modifies the pixels:

      a. A global anti-AI micro-watermark grid is applied across the whole
         image - a +/-3 luminance modulation on a 12 px lattice. Invisible to
         the eye, but it perturbs the statistical regularity that generative
         models rely on when regenerating an image.

      b. A stamp panel is filled across the bottom 18% of the frame, in cream
         at 92% opacity. A panel rather than a drop shadow, because text with
         only a shadow fails both on bright sky and on dark interiors.

      c. A left accent bar is drawn, its width scaled to the image width so it
         stays visible at full resolution.

      d. A dense dual-tone anti-AI pattern is drawn at full amplitude, confined
         to a strip below the text so it never fights legibility.

      e. Three text lines are drawn: place name, coordinates, and timestamp.
         Font size is derived as a percentage of image height (target 2.2%),
         never a fixed pixel size, so the stamp scales with the frame.

    The banner region matters: it is the region the perceptual hash is computed
    over. This is what makes edits to the stamp text detectable.

  STEP 3 - SIGN AND EMBED  (lib/services/security_service.dart)
    signAndEmbed() operates on the stamped image:

      a. Compute the perceptual hash (dHash) of the banner region.
      b. Resolve the active hardware-derived signing key.
      c. Build the envelope: lat, lon, alt, timestamp, device id, pixel hash,
         key id, schema version.
      d. Compute HMAC-SHA256 over the canonical serialisation of that envelope.
      e. Embed the resulting JSON in THREE places:
           - EXIF UserComment
           - a standards-compliant JPEG COM segment (0xFFFE) after SOI
           - an EOF payload appended after the end of the JPEG data
         Triple embedding is deliberate: different tools strip different
         things, and the EOF copy survives EXIF stripping entirely.

  STEP 4 - PERSIST
    The final bytes are written to a file named veripic_<millis>.png in the
    temporary directory, then exported to the device gallery under the album
    "VeriPic" via the gal package.

IMPORTANT INVARIANT: the bytes returned by capture() are final. They have
already been stamped, signed, and embedded. Re-signing them would append a
second payload and corrupt verification. This is noted at the call site.

6. HOW IT WORKS - THE VERIFICATION PIPELINE
===============================================================================

Implemented in lib/services/verification_service.dart, method verify().
It reports progress through a callback so the UI can animate the checklist.

  STAGE 1 - EXTRACT THE PAYLOAD
    extractEnvelope() attempts recovery in order of robustness:
      1. The EOF payload (most robust, and the only copy older builds produced
         reliably).
      2. A raw byte scan for the COM/EXIF marker - a single scan finds either,
         since both are plain bytes in the file.
      3. Decoder-assisted fallback via PNG text chunks and parsed EXIF.

    Because the COM and EXIF copies are found by scanning raw bytes, there is no
    delimiter marking where the JSON ends. A brace-balanced JSON extractor
    handles this, correctly skipping braces inside string literals and escapes.

    If nothing is found: verdict NOT SIGNED. Remaining stages are skipped, but
    AI screening still runs, so an unsigned image still gets an opinion.

  STAGE 2 - VALIDATE THE HMAC
    verifySignatureDetailed() checks the signature against the device key ring:
      1. The key named by the envelope's kid field (fast path for v4).
      2. The active hardware-derived key.
      3. Every remaining key in the ring, including the imported legacy secret.

    Comparison is constant-time to avoid leaking information through timing.

    If no key reproduces the signature: verdict TAMPERED METADATA. The report
    distinguishes "signed by a key this device does not hold" from "no key
    reproduces this signature", which are different situations.

  STAGE 3 - PERCEPTUAL COMPARISON
    Re-compute the banner dHash from the supplied image and compare it to the
    hash recorded in the envelope, by Hamming distance.

      distance <= 10  -> the banner is intact (JPEG recompression tolerated)
      distance >  10  -> verdict TAMPERED PIXELS

    The threshold of 10 bits out of 64 is the documented tolerance and is
    covered by a test.

  STAGE 4 - PHOTOGRAPHIC SCENE
    Re-compute the tiled scene hash and compare it tile-by-tile against the
    tiles recorded in the envelope.

      every tile within tolerance -> the picture is unaltered
      any tile over tolerance     -> verdict TAMPERED SCENE, reporting how many
                                     tiles moved and which ones

    Envelopes at v4 or earlier carry no scene tiles. This stage then reports
    "skipped - predates scene protection" rather than implying the scene passed,
    and the authentic verdict says so in words too.

  THE VERDICT IS BINARY

    There is deliberately no confidence percentage. A cryptographic signature
    either reproduces or it does not; a tile is either inside tolerance or
    outside it. Blending those into a single "87%" invited the reader to treat a
    failed signature as a strong-ish pass, which is precisely backwards. The
    report states the verdict, and lists the measurements that produced it -
    Hamming distance, tiles matched, which key verified - so a reader can judge
    for themselves.

7. CRYPTOGRAPHIC DESIGN
===============================================================================

PRIMITIVE:      HMAC-SHA256
KEY DERIVATION: HKDF-SHA256 (RFC 5869), implemented from scratch over
                package:crypto - extract-then-expand, no native dependency.

KEY DERIVATION PARAMETERS
    Key = HKDF-SHA256(
        IKM  = <hardware device seed>,
        Salt = "VeriPic-Device-Salt-2026",
        Info = "HMAC-Image-Signing",
        L    = 32 bytes
    )

The derived key is cached in FlutterSecureStorage, which is backed by the
Android Keystore. Because derivation is deterministic, the key can always be
regenerated from the hardware seed even if secure storage fails.

THE KEY RING
Rather than a single key, the device holds a ring. This exists so that rotating
the hardware-derived key - after a factory reset, a device swap, or migration
off the old scheme - never orphans photos that were already signed.
Verification walks the entire ring.

Each key carries:
    kid         first 16 hex chars of SHA-256 over the key material
    material    raw HMAC key bytes
    origin      hardwareDerived | legacyRandom
    createdAt   epoch millis

BACKWARD COMPATIBILITY
Envelopes signed before the HKDF migration used a per-install random secret
(v3). That secret is imported into the ring once, marked verify-only, and never
used for new signatures. Old photos therefore remain verifiable forever.

Note the subtlety handled in _readLegacyKey(): v3 signed with
Hmac(sha256, utf8.encode(secret)), so the legacy key material is the UTF-8 bytes
of the stored string, not its base64 decoding. Reproducing this exactly is what
makes old signatures still validate.

DOWNGRADE RESISTANCE
The canonical string bound by the HMAC differs by version:

    v3 and earlier:  "v1|lat|lon|alt|ts|device|pixelhash"
    v4:              "v4|lat|lon|alt|ts|device|pixelhash|kid"
    v5:              "v5|lat|lon|alt|ts|device|pixelhash|tile,tile,...|kid"

Because v4 binds the key id into the canonical form, an attacker cannot strip
the kid field to force the verifier down the legacy path - removing it changes
the canonical string and therefore invalidates the signature. There is a
dedicated test for this: "stripping the key id does not downgrade to a
forgeable canonical form".


===============================================================================
8. PERCEPTUAL HASHING AND TAMPER DETECTION
===============================================================================

ALGORITHM: Difference hash (dHash), 64-bit, computed over the banner region.

    1. Crop the bottom 18% of the image - the stamp banner.
    2. Resize that crop to 9x8 pixels using average interpolation.
    3. For each of the 8 rows, compare each pixel's luminance to its right
       neighbour, producing 8 bits per row = 64 bits total.
       Luminance uses the standard ITU-R weighting:
           Y = 0.299*R + 0.587*G + 0.114*B
    4. Serialise as 16 hex characters.

WHY dHASH AND WHY THE BANNER ONLY

A cryptographic hash of the whole file would be useless here: re-saving a JPEG
at a different quality changes every byte, and legitimate photos get recompressed
constantly by messaging apps and email clients.

dHash records the *direction of brightness gradients* rather than absolute
values. Recompression barely perturbs gradient direction, so the hash is stable
across quality changes - but moving, editing, or overwriting text in the banner
changes many gradients at once and flips many bits.

TILED SCENE HASHING (v5 onward)

The banner hash alone left the photographic content unprotected. From envelope
schema v5 the scene above the banner is ALSO fingerprinted, as a 4x4 grid of
independent 64-bit dHashes - sixteen tiles, row-major.

Why a grid rather than one hash of the whole scene: a single 64-bit digest
averaged over an entire frame is far too coarse to catch a localised edit.
Cloning out one object barely moves it. Hashing each tile separately keeps the
edit confined to the tiles it actually touched, where it is unmistakable - and
it lets the UI show you *where* the frame changed, not merely that it did.

    Grid:       4 x 4 = 16 tiles over the top 82% of the frame
    Per tile:   64-bit dHash, 16 hex chars
    Tolerance:  10 bits per tile
    Verdict:    any tile over tolerance -> tamperedScene

All sixteen tile hashes are bound into the HMAC canonical string, so they cannot
be stripped to escape the check - removing them changes the canonical form and
invalidates the signature. There is a test for this.

Remaining limitation: an edit smaller than a tile may not move that tile's hash
past the tolerance. The scheme detects substantial edits reliably and very small
ones unreliably.

SERIALISATION NOTE (bug fixed 22 August 2026)

Digests are serialised as UNSIGNED 64-bit hex, built from two 32-bit halves.

An earlier implementation used `dHash.toRadixString(16).padLeft(16,'0')`. Since
`1 << 63` sets Dart's sign bit, any digest whose top bit was set serialised as
"-8000000000000000" - seventeen characters, and negative. Fed into the distance
comparison, the loop `while (xorVal > BigInt.zero)` terminated immediately and
returned distance 0, so two genuinely different hashes compared as IDENTICAL.
That was a silent false "no tampering detected" affecting roughly half of all
comparisons. The comparison now runs a fixed 64 iterations and normalises the
historical signed spelling, so photos signed before the fix still compare
correctly.

COMPARISON: Hamming distance over the 64-bit values.
TOLERANCE:  10 bits. Above this, the verdict is tamperedPixels.

Edge cases are handled defensively: empty inputs, differing lengths, and
unparseable hex all return 64 (maximally distant) rather than throwing or
falsely matching.

ANTI-AI MICRO-PATTERNING
Two layers, both applied during capture:
    - A global +/-3 luminance grid on a 12 px lattice across the entire image.
    - A dense dual-tone pattern at full amplitude in a strip within the banner.
These disrupt the statistical smoothness that generative models produce, making
a regenerated or inpainted version of the image statistically distinguishable
from an authentic capture.

9. DEVICE IDENTITY
===============================================================================

Implemented in lib/services/device_service.dart.

The fingerprint is split into three deliberately separate tiers:

    seed        Secret hardware key material. Never leaves the device, never
                appears in an envelope, never shown in the UI. Used only as
                HKDF input keying material.

    publicId    A domain-separated SHA-256 of the seed, using the prefix
                "VeriPic-PublicDeviceID-v1". This is what gets embedded in every
                signed photo. Because it is derived through a distinct domain
                separator, publishing it does not reveal the HKDF input.

    attributes  Human-readable diagnostics for the UI. Includes volatile values
                that are intentionally EXCLUDED from key material.

WHY NOT IMEI
Reading the real IMEI is not viable for a normal app. Since Android 10 (API 29),
TelephonyManager.getImei() requires READ_PRIVILEGED_PHONE_STATE, which ordinary
Play Store apps cannot hold - it throws SecurityException rather than returning
a value. iOS never exposes IMEI to third-party apps at all. VeriPic therefore
uses the strongest legally accessible hardware-bound identifier per platform:

    Android:  Settings.Secure.ANDROID_ID  (stable for the install lifetime,
              survives reinstall, tied to device + signing key, no permission)
    iOS:      identifierForVendor         (Apple's sanctioned stable per-vendor
                                           device id, no permission)

WHY build.fingerprint IS NOT IN THE KEY MATERIAL
Build.FINGERPRINT changes on every OTA system update. Binding the signing key to
it would silently rotate the key after any system update and invalidate every
previously signed photo on that device. The seed therefore uses only OTA-stable
hardware attributes - brand, model, device, board, hardware, manufacturer on
Android; model, machine, sysname on iOS - and these are sorted before joining so
the seed is order-independent and reproducible. The build fingerprint is
retained purely as a display-level diagnostic.

FALLBACK
If no OS identifier is available (unsupported platform, emulator quirk), a
one-time random id is generated and persisted in the Keystore/Keychain so the
app still behaves deterministically. This case is flagged prominently in the UI,
because frames signed under a fallback key stay valid only on that install.

CACHING
The fingerprint is process-cached, and concurrent callers share a single
in-flight resolution, so a capture burst never hits the platform channels twice.

10. THE SIGNED ENVELOPE FORMAT
===============================================================================

The payload is compact JSON, prefixed by a marker so it can be located by a raw
byte scan.

    MARKERS
        COM / EXIF copy:  VPIC_METADATA:
        EOF copy:         VPIC_PAYLOAD:

    FIELDS
        lat    double   latitude, decimal degrees
        lon    double   longitude, decimal degrees
        alt    double   altitude, metres
        ts     int      capture time, epoch milliseconds UTC
        dev    string   public device hash
        ph     string   banner dHash, 16 unsigned hex chars
        st     array    16 scene tile dHashes (absent in v4 and earlier)
        sig    string   HMAC-SHA256, hex
        kid    string   signing key id (absent in v3 and earlier)
        v      int      schema version (currently 5)

    EXAMPLE
        VPIC_PAYLOAD:{"lat":15.8497,"lon":74.4977,"alt":610.2,
        "ts":1755772800000,"dev":"a3f9...","ph":"9c3e1f70a2b4d8e6",
        "sig":"7b2a...","kid":"3d9f1a8c2e4b6079","v":4}

PARSING TOLERANCE
    - A missing "v" is treated as version 3.
    - Coordinates arriving as integers rather than doubles are coerced.
    - Missing string fields default to empty rather than throwing.
This tolerance is what lets the current build read envelopes written by every
historical version of the app.

12. APPLICATION ARCHITECTURE
===============================================================================

Three layers, with a deliberately enforced boundary between them.

    lib/main.dart          Entry point. Loads .env, enumerates cameras, loads
                           the persisted theme choice, starts the app.

    lib/screens/           Presentation only. Widgets, layout, state of the
                           view. 7 files.

    lib/services/          All I/O and domain logic: camera, GPS, crypto,
                           device identity, image compositing, network,
                           storage. 6 files.

    lib/theme/             Design tokens, palettes, and shared UI primitives.
                           3 files.

    test/                  Unit tests. 2 files, 19 tests.

Total: 18 Dart files, roughly 5,264 lines in lib/.

STATE MANAGEMENT
Deliberately minimal - plain StatefulWidget with setState, plus one
ValueNotifier for the theme. No Provider, Riverpod, BLoC, or GetX. For an app of
this size the ceremony of a state management package would exceed its benefit.

THE PRESENTATION BOUNDARY
During the UI work a hard rule was established: presentation changes must not
touch services, data models, permission logic, GPS acquisition, camera capture,
EXIF writing, the stamp compositing pipeline, navigation route names, or tests.
This is why several known improvements are listed as outstanding rather than
done - they require crossing that line and need explicit sign-off.

Known coupling that violates clean layering, documented but not yet extracted:
    - camera_screen.dart subscribes to Geolocator directly and performs reverse
      geocoding in the widget.
    - GPS quality policy (15 m weak, 30 s stale) lives in the presentation layer.
    - home_screen.dart instantiates DeviceService and SecurityService directly.
    - verify_screen.dart reads files and drives VerificationService from the
      widget.

13. FILE-BY-FILE REFERENCE
===============================================================================

--- ENTRY POINT ---

lib/main.dart
    Initialises the Flutter binding, loads .env (optional - services fall back
    to safe defaults), enumerates available cameras, loads the persisted theme
    mode, and runs the app. Wraps MaterialApp in a ValueListenableBuilder so a
    theme change is a rebuild rather than a restart.

--- SERVICES ---

lib/services/camera_service.dart
    Owns the CameraController. initialize() requests camera and location
    permissions and throws a PermissionRequiredException carrying enough context
    for the UI to show a helpful prompt. capture() runs the full pipeline of
    section 5. Also provides focusAt() and setFlashMode(), both of which fail
    silently on sensors that do not support them so the UI stays responsive.

lib/services/device_service.dart
    Produces the DeviceFingerprint - seed, publicId, label, attributes, and the
    usedFallback flag. Process-cached with shared in-flight resolution.
    Fully documented in section 9.

lib/services/security_service.dart
    The cryptographic core, and the largest service (~731 lines). Contains:
      - hkdfSha256()             RFC 5869 implementation
      - activeKey() / keyRing()  key management and persistence
      - signAndEmbed()           signing and triple embedding
      - extractEnvelope()        multi-strategy payload recovery
      - computeBannerDHash()     perceptual hashing
      - hammingDistance()        bit-difference comparison
      - verifySignatureDetailed() ring-walking verification
      - _canonical()             version-aware canonical serialisation
      - _constantTimeEq()        timing-safe comparison

lib/services/overlay_service.dart
    Burns the visible stamp into the pixels, and applies both anti-AI pattern
    layers. Also performs reverse geocoding for the stamp text, with a fallback
    string if the geocoder is unavailable.

lib/services/verification_service.dart
    Orchestrates the four-stage verification, emits progress for the UI, and
    reports the verdict. Defines VerifyStage, StageState, and
    VerificationReport.

lib/services/certificate_service.dart
    Renders the section 63(4) evidence certificate as a PDF. Collects the
    technical particulars from the report, the envelope, and the device
    fingerprint; leaves both statutory signature blocks blank; and prints an
    explicit limitations paragraph that adapts to whether the frame carried
    scene protection.

lib/services/frame_store.dart
    Read-only index of frames this app has written. Enumerates files matching
    veripic_<millis>.png, parses the capture time from the filename, and returns
    them newest-first. Deliberately scoped to VeriPic's own output - it never
    reads the device camera roll. Does not change the capture pipeline; it only
    reads what that pipeline has already produced.

--- SCREENS ---

lib/screens/app_shell.dart
    Bottom-tab shell hosting Home, Frames, and Locations in an IndexedStack, so
    tab state survives switching. Owns the app bar and the theme toggle.

lib/screens/home_screen.dart
    Display headline, primary "Open viewfinder" action, a two-card tool grid,
    and the device identity card with its expandable diagnostics.

lib/screens/camera_screen.dart
    The viewfinder. The largest screen. Manages camera lifecycle, the GPS

15. DESIGN SYSTEM
===============================================================================

The design system is specified in flutter_app/CLAUDE.md, which is the source of
truth - code derives from it, not the other way round.

CURRENT DIRECTION: "Bold paper"
Flat cream ground, heavy black outlines, hard offset shadows, saturated colour
used as identity. Every surface reads like a physical card on a desk: outlined,
slightly raised, unambiguous. Nothing subtle, nothing glassy.

COLOUR
    canvas          #FCF9F0   app background
    surface         #FFFFFF   cards, sheets
    surface-inset   #F1EDE1   inset wells, disabled fills
    outline         #000000   every border, divider, shadow
    text            #000000   primary text
    text-soft       #6B6B6B   secondary text and metadata
    accent          #FFD84D   primary action - shutter, confirm
    accent-press    #E8C22F   pressed accent
    ok              #5EE9A0   verified, authentic, signature valid
    alert           #FF7BA0   tampered, denied, degraded GPS
    warn            #F5E3B0   caution, fallback key, unavailable service
    info            #A78BFA   category identity
    cool            #C8E85A   category identity
    null            #C4C4C4   unsorted, empty

Colour is identity, not decoration - it says which thing this is, or what state
it is in. ok, alert, and warn are reserved for state and may never be used as
category identity.

TYPE
Two bundled faces. Space Grotesk is a variable font; weight rides the wght axis
rather than a faux-bold.

    Role          Size  Weight  Family    Tracking
    Display        30    700    Grotesk   -0.02em
    Screen title   22    700    Grotesk   -0.01em
    Card title     15    700    Grotesk    0
    Body           15    400    Grotesk    0
    Label          12    500    Grotesk    0.02em
    Data           12    400    Mono       0
    Data small     10    400    Mono       0.06em, uppercase

THE MONOSPACE RULE
Every number a user might verify renders in JetBrains Mono: coordinates,
accuracy, timestamps, hashes, counts, altitude. Prose renders in Space Grotesk.
The rationale is stated in the spec: a coordinate in a proportional font
instantly looks untrustworthy. This rule has survived every redesign.

GEOMETRY
    Radius:   16px cards and sheets, 12px buttons/inputs/icon tiles, full round
              on badges only. Nothing else.
    Borders:  2px solid outline everywhere; 3px only on the shutter.
    Shadow:   4px 4px 0 outline - hard, zero blur, never coloured, never soft.
              The only depth cue in the system.
    Press:    pressed elements drop the shadow and translate 2px, 2px, so the
              press feels physical.
    Spacing:  4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32. Nothing between.
    Touch:    minimum 48dp. Field users wear gloves.

16. THEMING (LIGHT AND DARK)
===============================================================================

Implemented as a Palette ThemeExtension, so colours resolve from BuildContext
rather than being compile-time constants. A theme change is a rebuild, not a
restart.

                    LIGHT       DARK
    canvas          #FCF9F0     #15130F
    surface         #FFFFFF     #23201A
    surface-inset   #F1EDE1     #2E2A22
    outline         #000000     #000000
    text            #000000     #F5F1E6
    text-soft       #6B6B6B     #A39C8C

TWO DELIBERATE DECISIONS

  1. Dark is NOT an inversion. The outline stays pure black in both themes.
     Inverting it to white would destroy the ink-on-paper metaphor the whole
     system rests on - white outlines read as neon, not as printed card.

  2. Identity and state colours do not change between themes, and their glyphs
     stay black in both. Every one of accent, ok, alert, warn, info, cool and
     null is a light pigment, so black is the only legible ink on them. The
     token Tokens.onIdentity exists precisely for this - using textPrimary would
     put cream text on a yellow shutter in dark mode.

MODE SELECTION
Three modes - system, light, dark - cycled from the app bar button and persisted
in secure storage. System is the default, and light remains the fallback when
the system expresses no preference, because the app is used outdoors.

    26 unit tests across 2 files, all passing.

    test/security_service_test.dart - cryptographic correctness
      HKDF-SHA256:
        - matches RFC 5869 Test Case 1
        - matches Test Case 3 (empty salt and info)
        - is deterministic for the VeriPic parameters
        - a different hardware seed yields a different key
      Signing keys:
        - key id is a stable 16-hex-char digest of the material
        - survives a JSON round-trip
      Hamming distance:
        - identical hashes score zero
        - counts differing bits
        - mismatched or empty inputs are treated as maximally distant
        - the tamper threshold is the documented 10 bits

    test/envelope_roundtrip_test.dart - end-to-end integrity
        - signs, embeds and recovers an envelope end to end
        - embeds a standards-compliant JPEG COM segment
        - recovers the envelope from the COM copy when the EOF tail is stripped
        - rejects an envelope whose payload was edited
        - stripping the key id does not downgrade to a forgeable canonical form
        - a legacy v3 random-secret signature still verifies
      Scene protection:
        - tiles the scene and binds them into the signature
        - an edit confined to one tile moves only that tile
        - mismatched tile sets are not comparable
        - stripping the scene tiles invalidates the signature
      Hash serialisation:
        - a top-bit digest is unsigned and 16 chars
        - the legacy signed form still compares correctly
        - all-bits-different scores 64
        - v4 round-trips through JSON
        - parses a legacy v3 envelope with no key id
        - tolerates integer-typed coordinates and a missing version

    Validating HKDF against the published RFC 5869 vectors matters: it proves
    the from-scratch implementation is genuinely RFC-conformant rather than
    merely self-consistent.

20a. DISTRIBUTION AND HOSTING
===============================================================================

CLOUDFLARE

  DO NOT host the APK on Cloudflare Pages. Pages enforces a 25 MiB limit per
  static asset, and a Flutter release APK exceeds that comfortably once the
  engine, both bundled fonts, and the PDF library are included. The upload will
  be rejected outright.

  Use CLOUDFLARE R2 instead - object storage, with no per-file limit of that
  kind. Typical arrangement:

    1. Create an R2 bucket for release artefacts.
    2. Upload the APK (wrangler r2 object put, or the dashboard).
    3. Expose it either through a custom domain bound to the bucket, or via a
       Worker that proxies the object.
    4. Serve with:
         Content-Type: application/vnd.android.package-archive
       so that browsers download it rather than trying to render it.

  A reasonable split is to keep the landing page on Pages - it is small, static,
  and gets Pages' CDN for free - and point its download button at the R2 URL.
  Pages for the page, R2 for the payload.

  Note that a sideloaded APK gives no update channel: users must download and
  install each new build by hand, and Android will warn them about installing
  from an unknown source. That is acceptable for pilot distribution to a known
  group, and poor for anything wider.

GOOGLE PLAY

  Play does not accept APKs for new applications. You must upload an Android App
  Bundle:

      flutter build appbundle --release

  Before the first upload:

    * Change the application id. It is currently com.example.veripic, and Play
      rejects anything in the com.example namespace.
    * Set up release signing. There is currently no signing configuration in
      android/app/build.gradle.kts, so release builds are debug-signed and
      cannot be published.
    * Enrol in Play App Signing.

  Store listing obligations that this app specifically triggers:

    * A privacy policy is mandatory, because the app accesses location and
      camera.
    * The Data safety form must be completed. Answer it accurately: VeriPic
      collects location and photographs and processes them ENTIRELY on the
      device. No photograph and no coordinate is transmitted anywhere. The only
      network traffic the app generates is fetching OpenStreetMap basemap tiles
      for the Locations screen, which sends the viewed map area - not the
      photographs - to the tile server.
    * Location permission justification: the app needs precise location because
      the coordinate is the substance of the record, not an enhancement to it.
    * If distributing in India and marketing the section 63(4) export as legal
      evidence, have the wording reviewed. Claiming court admissibility is a
      legal claim, and the certificate is a draft requiring signature (see
      section 21).

  Target API level requirements move every year; check Play's current minimum
  before each release.

21. SECURITY POSTURE - WHAT IT PROVES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
===============================================================================

This section matters more than any other. Be precise about the claims.

WHAT VERIPIC PROVES

  * That the metadata (coordinates, altitude, timestamp, device) has not been
    altered since capture. Any edit breaks the HMAC.

  * That the visible stamp banner has not been edited. Any meaningful change to
    the stamp text moves the dHash beyond the tolerance.

  * (v5 onward) That the photographic content has not been substantially
    edited. Sixteen tile fingerprints cover the scene above the banner, and the
    report identifies which tiles moved.

  * That the position was not reported by a mock location provider, since
    capture is refused outright in that case.

  * That the photo was signed by a key held by a specific device.

  * That the payload survived transport, thanks to triple embedding.

WHAT VERIPIC DOES NOT PROVE

  * IT DOES NOT DETECT ARBITRARILY SMALL EDITS TO THE PHOTOGRAPH.
    Scene protection works at tile resolution - one sixteenth of the frame.
    A substantial edit is caught reliably; an edit small relative to a tile may
    not move that tile's hash past tolerance. And frames signed at v4 or earlier
    have no scene coverage at all; the report says so explicitly rather than
    implying they passed.

  * IT DOES NOT PROVE THE PHOTO SHOWS WHAT THE GPS SAYS.
    The app records the device's reported location and now refuses to sign a fix
    the operating system flags as mocked (Position.isMocked). That defeats
    ordinary fake-GPS apps. It does NOT defeat a device whose operating system
    or firmware has been modified to report a false position without setting the
    flag, nor GNSS signal spoofing at the radio level.

  * IT IS NOT A PUBLIC-KEY SYSTEM.
    HMAC is symmetric. Verification requires possession of the key, which means
    in practice a photo can only be fully verified on the device that captured
    it, or on a device holding that key. A third party cannot independently
    verify a VeriPic photo without the secret. Moving to an asymmetric scheme
    (Ed25519 sign / public verify) is the single most valuable upgrade
    available and is listed in the roadmap.

  * IT DOES NOT PROTECT AGAINST A COMPROMISED DEVICE.
    If an attacker extracts the derived key from the Keystore, they can forge
    signatures for that device indefinitely. There is no revocation.

  * ANDROID_ID IS NOT UNSPOOFABLE.
    It is the strongest identifier an ordinary app can reach, but on a rooted
    device it can be changed.

  * THE EVIDENCE CERTIFICATE IS A DRAFT, NOT A LEGAL INSTRUMENT.
    The section 63(4) export sets out accurately what the app verified and by
    what method. It is unsigned by design: the statute requires signature by the
    person in lawful control of the device and by a person of expert capacity,
    and neither is the app. Unsigned it has no legal effect. Nothing in this
    project constitutes legal advice, and no representation is made that any
    court will admit the record. Have the wording reviewed by a qualified lawyer
    in the relevant jurisdiction before relying on it.

KEY MANAGEMENT NOTE
The signing key never leaves the device and is never transmitted. The public
device hash embedded in photos is domain-separated from the HKDF input, so
publishing it does not weaken the key.

OUTSTANDING SECURITY ITEM
A live NVIDIA API key was committed to .env.example in commit f23baa1 and
REMAINS IN THE GIT HISTORY. The AI subsystem, the .env file and .env.example
have all since been deleted, but deleting a file does not remove it from
history. THAT KEY SHOULD STILL BE REVOKED at build.nvidia.com — anyone with a
clone of this repository can recover it. A vestigial APP_SIGNING_SECRET was also
present and has been removed; it was unused after the HKDF migration.

22. KNOWN ISSUES AND LIMITATIONS
===============================================================================

  1. THE STAMP DOES NOT RENDER IN JETBRAINS MONO.
     The design system requires the stamp to be composited using the bundled
     mono font at 2.2% of image height. package:image can only draw its own
     bitmap fonts - it cannot rasterise a TTF at all. The current code
     approximates by selecting between arial24 and arial48 based on image
     height. Doing this properly requires recompositing on a dart:ui Canvas at
     native resolution, which changes the capture pipeline.

  2. CAPTURED FRAMES LIVE IN THE TEMPORARY DIRECTORY.
     Android can evict them under storage pressure, which would thin out the
     Frames gallery and the Locations plot. The permanent copies remain in the
     device's VeriPic album. Fixing this means moving captures to a documents
     directory in camera_service.dart.

  3. THE MAP NEEDS NETWORK.
     Basemap tiles are fetched from OpenStreetMap, so with no signal the pins
     and the list still work but the map renders on a blank background. Tile
     caching for offline field use is not implemented.

  4. GPS SPOOFING IS ONLY PARTLY DEFEATED.
     Mock-provider fixes are now refused, but a modified OS or radio-level GNSS
     spoofing is still not detected. See section 21.

  5. SCENE PROTECTION WORKS AT TILE RESOLUTION.
     Substantial edits are caught; edits small relative to one sixteenth of the
     frame may not be. Frames signed at v4 or earlier have no scene coverage.

  6. NO WIDGET OR INTEGRATION TESTS.

  7. RENDERING AT 320dp AND 200% FONT SCALE IS UNVERIFIED.

  8. iOS IS IMPLEMENTED IN CODE BUT UNTESTED.
     device_service.dart handles identifierForVendor and iOS attributes, but
     the app has only ever been built and run for Android.

  9. THE STAMP PANEL COLOUR PREDATES THE CURRENT PALETTE.
     It uses the older cream value and has not been re-checked against the
     current "Bold paper" spec.

 10. NO EXIF GPS TAGS ARE WRITTEN.
     The spec calls for writing GPS to EXIF as well as burning it into pixels
     ("the pixels are for humans, EXIF is for everything downstream"). Currently
     coordinates live only in the signed envelope and the visible banner.

 11. THE ORIGINAL UNSTAMPED FRAME IS NOT RETAINED.
     The spec calls for keeping it so a different stamp template could be
     applied to an old photo later.

24. PROJECT HISTORY
===============================================================================

The project has passed through several distinct phases:

  1. INITIAL BUILD - capture, stamping, HMAC signing with a per-install random
     secret, dual embedding, and a basic verification screen.

  2. HARDWARE KEY BINDING - the random secret was replaced by an HKDF-SHA256 key
     derived from the device's hardware identifier, with a key ring added so
     previously signed photos stayed verifiable. Envelope schema went to v4 with
     downgrade resistance.

  3. UI DIRECTION ONE - "Cyber forensics": dark OLED background, neon emerald
     accents, glassmorphism, animated backdrops. Rejected as overdesigned.

  4. UI DIRECTION TWO - light, flat, blue-accented, minimal. An intermediate
     step, superseded.

  5. UI DIRECTION THREE - "Field kit": warm sand ground, forest green, a single
     orange signal colour, Space Grotesk and JetBrains Mono, 6px radius,
     hairline borders. This is when CLAUDE.md was introduced as the design
     source of truth and when the two fonts were bundled.

  6. UI DIRECTION FOUR - "Bold paper", the current system: cream ground, heavy
     black outlines, hard offset shadows, saturated identity colours, 16px
     radii. CLAUDE.md was rewritten to match rather than letting code contradict
     the spec.

  7. STRUCTURED REFACTOR - a phased overhaul introduced a strict token layer, a
     primitives layer with pressed/disabled/focused states defined up front, and
     a hard presentation/services boundary.

  8. NAVIGATION AND THEMING - dark theme via ThemeExtension, bottom-tab
     navigation, the Frames gallery, the Locations plot, and a fix for the
     camera permission race.

  9. EVIDENTIARY HARDENING (22 August 2026) - mock-location capture blocking,
     tiled scene fingerprinting at envelope v5, the section 63(4) certificate
     export, and a fix to a latent hash-serialisation bug that could report two
     different digests as identical.

 10. SIMPLIFICATION AND MAPPING (22 August 2026) - the NVIDIA AI screening
     subsystem was removed entirely, along with flutter_dotenv and the .env
     file, leaving an app that needs no third-party account and transmits no
     photograph anywhere. The confidence percentage was dropped in favour of a
     binary verdict. The Locations coordinate plot was replaced by a real
     interactive OpenStreetMap map with themed markers and animated
     fly-to-capture.

A note on the design churn: three of the four UI directions were discarded.
The cost of that was reduced substantially by the token layer - because tokens
are named by role (surface, textPrimary, statusAlert) rather than appearance,
the entire palette could be swapped without renaming call sites. The one
exception was borderHairline, which had to become outline when the border went
from 1px to 2px, because the old name had become a lie.


===============================================================================
24a. THE GEOGUARD RENAME
===============================================================================

The app was renamed from VeriPic to GeoGuard on 22 August 2026. The rename was
applied to everything a user or a court can see, and deliberately NOT applied to
four categories of string, because each is load-bearing:

  HKDF salt              'VeriPic-Device-Salt-2026'
      This string is HKDF input keying material. Changing it derives a
      different signing key, which would invalidate every photograph already
      signed on every device.

  Device id domain       'VeriPic-PublicDeviceID-v1'
      Hashed into the published device identifier that goes into every
      envelope. Changing it changes every device hash, and therefore every
      signature.

  Payload markers        'VPIC_METADATA:' / 'VPIC_PAYLOAD:'
      The byte sequences used to locate the payload inside an image file.
      Changing them makes existing signed photographs unreadable.

  Secure storage keys    'veripic_key_ring_v4', 'veripic_hw_signing_key_v3',
                         'veripic_fallback_hw_id'
      Changing them orphans the stored key ring on every existing install.

A product name is a label; these are part of the wire format and the key
derivation. They are commented in the source to stop a future contributor
"tidying" them.

Two further points about the rename:

  * The capture filename prefix DID change, from `veripic_<millis>.png` to
    `geoguard_<millis>.png`. FrameStore matches both, so frames captured before
    the rename still appear in the gallery.

  * The signing certificate's subject still reads CN=VeriPic. It is cosmetic and
    invisible in normal use, but it can only be changed by generating a new
    keystore - which changes the signing key, and therefore ANDROID_ID, and
    therefore every derived HMAC key. It must be decided before the first public
    release or never.

===============================================================================
25. GLOSSARY
===============================================================================

    dHash                Difference hash. A perceptual hash recording the
                         direction of brightness gradients between adjacent
                         pixels. Survives recompression; breaks on edits.

    Hamming distance     The number of differing bits between two equal-length
                         values. Here, between two 64-bit dHashes.

    HMAC                 Hash-based Message Authentication Code. Proves both
                         integrity and authenticity, using a shared secret.

    HKDF                 HMAC-based Key Derivation Function, RFC 5869. Turns
                         input keying material into one or more strong keys via
                         an extract step then an expand step.

    IKM                  Input Keying Material - the raw secret fed into HKDF.

    Envelope             The JSON payload embedded in a signed photo, carrying
                         the claim and its signature.

    kid                  Key id. First 16 hex chars of SHA-256 over the key
                         material; identifies which key signed an envelope.

    Key ring             The set of keys a device can verify with: the active
                         hardware-derived key plus retained historical keys.

    COM segment          A JPEG comment segment, marker 0xFFFE. A standard place
                         to store arbitrary text in a JPEG.

    EOF payload          Data appended after the end of the JPEG stream. Ignored
                         by decoders, survives EXIF stripping.

    Domain separation    Deriving different values from the same secret using
                         distinct prefixes, so exposing one does not compromise
                         another.

    Constant-time compare A comparison whose duration does not depend on where
                         two values first differ, preventing timing attacks.

    ANDROID_ID           Settings.Secure.ANDROID_ID. A per-device, per-signing-
                         key identifier, stable across reinstalls.

    ThemeExtension       Flutter mechanism for attaching custom values to a
                         ThemeData so they resolve from BuildContext.

    Reduced motion       An OS accessibility setting requesting minimal
                         animation. Honoured via Tokens.motion().
